About this guide

This guide follows a visit through Mp10 from start to finish. The patient is registered, their insurance card is scanned, they sign, an encounter is opened and what they came for is ordered. The biller turns that encounter into a claim, codes it and sends it. The payer answers. The answer is posted, and whatever is still owed is worked until it is paid.

It is written for the people who do that work every day:

  • Front desk and admissions staff — Part I, Patients and encounters.
  • Billers and coders — Part II, Claims, coding and collections.
  • Payment posters — Part III, Remittances and payments.

Each part can be read on its own, but each one hands over to the next, and the reasons behind many of the steps only make sense further along. The insurance card you mark as primary at the front desk is the one the claim bills. The order you enter on the encounter is the line the biller starts from. The remittance you post whole teaches the system which codes a payer refuses. It is worth reading the parts you do not do yourself, at least once.

How the screens behave

A few habits are the same on every grid in Mp10:

  • Click a column header to sort by it. The active header turns red.
  • Type while a grid has focus to jump to the first row that starts with what you typed, in the column the grid is sorted by. What you have typed shows in the Incremental Search box on the left.
  • F8 (or Ctrl+F) opens the Search Dialog. It jumps to the first match in the sorted column. It does not hide the other rows.
  • The Filter panel on the left hides rows. It works only while the filter is switched on: click the LED or the word Filter to turn it on and off.
  • Enter or F7 opens the highlighted row. Insert or F5 adds a new one.
  • Right-click a grid for printing, Excel, Change Font, and Save Current Grid State, which keeps your column widths and order for next time.

Part I — Patients and encounters

Finding and registering the patient, their ID, signature and insurance cards, opening the encounter, ordering what they came for, and seeing which visits are still waiting to be billed.

Finding a patient

Open the patients list with the Patients Table button on the toolbar (or Tables → Patients). The Patients Data Grid lists every patient, sorted by name.

There are three ways to find someone:

  1. Type the name. With the grid sorted by Name, type the last name followed by the first: GARCIA MARIA. The grid jumps as you type. It matches from the start of the name, so you do not need to type all of it.
  2. Search (F8). Sort by the column you know — MedRec, SSN, Name, DOB, City, Insurance or Creation — then press F8 and type the value. For a record number you do not need the leading zeros; the search adds them.
  3. Filter. In the Filter panel on the left, fill in any of Last, Name, SSN, City, a range of birth dates (dob from, dob thru) or a range of creation dates, and switch the filter on. Last, first name and SSN match anywhere in the field, so ROD finds RODRIGUEZ and RODNEY alike. The number of patients found shows under the search box.

You cannot search by phone number.

Small icons in the grid tell you more about a patient before you open them:

Icon Where Means Click it to
Paperclip or note MedRec The patient has attachments or notes Open the attachments
Tag Name The patient has tags —
271 (green or red) Insurance An eligibility answer is on file, accepted or not Open the eligibility window

Possible duplicate patients

Two records for the same person cause trouble for years: the history is split, claims go out under two record numbers, and payments land on the wrong one. Mp10 catches most duplicates when the patient is entered (next chapter), and the dashboard’s Patient records card lists the ones that slipped through as possible duplicate patients: records with the same last name, first name, birth date and city. From that list, highlight the record to keep and press Merge group….

A record number can also be changed or merged by hand with Chng Record in the patients grid’s side panel. Mp10 merges two records only when the name, date of birth and SSN all match; otherwise it refuses.

Adding and editing a patient

Opening the patient window

  • New patient: press Insert (or F5), or click New Patient in the side panel.
  • Existing patient: double-click the row, or press Enter.

The patient window has these tabs in PrBill10: Demographics, Insurance, Encounters, Claims, Letters and Pre-Auths. The calendar shows only the first three.

At the bottom are Save and Exit. Save keeps the window open, so you can carry on with the insurance and the encounter. Ctrl+S saves and closes in one step; Ctrl+E exits.

Institutional: Patients10 adds an Address tab (postal and physical address, employer) and shows the encounters and claims of the patient read-only on their tabs.

The Demographics tab

Type the patient’s details: Last Name, First Name, SSN, DOB, address, phones, e-mail, marital information and an emergency contact.

  • Last Name holds both surnames (RIVERA LOPEZ). There is no separate second-surname field.
  • Names and the SSN are kept in capitals. Mp10 removes anything that is not a letter, a space or a hyphen from a name when you save.
  • City and Zip have a search button. Pick either one and the other two (city, state, zip) fill in together.
  • Phone numbers start with the practice’s area code.
  • Age is worked out from the date of birth.

Only the last name is required. A future date of birth is refused with Invalid DOB, and an e-mail address that does not look like one asks you to confirm.

The record number

The record number (MedRec) is given when you first save the patient, not when you open the window. You cannot type it. Once saved, the window’s title shows it.

Several things are unavailable until the patient has been saved once, because they need the record number: the insurance cards, the HIPAA signature, the encounters and tags. Save the patient first, then carry on.

When Mp10 thinks the patient already exists

On a new patient, as you leave the name, SSN or date-of-birth fields, Mp10 looks for someone already on file with the same SSN, or a similar name. If it finds one, a window shows that patient’s name, SSN, date of birth and address, with two buttons:

  • Jump to Existing Pte closes the new record and takes you to the one on file. Choose this when it is the same person.
  • Continue W/New Pte carries on with the new record. Choose this only when you are sure it is someone else — two relatives with similar names, for instance.

Your administrator can turn this check off, but should not.

Saving and the primary insurance

When you save a patient who has insurance cards, one of them must be marked Primary. If none is, Mp10 refuses with Can’t save: no primary insurance and asks you to open the card that should be primary, tick Primary, save it, and save the patient again.

When the patient is saved, the primary card’s insurance becomes the Insurance shown for the patient in the grid and on reports.

The patient’s photo ID

The ID box on the Demographics tab holds a picture of the patient’s driver’s licence or ID card. It is how anyone in the practice, now or later, can confirm the person in front of them is the patient on file.

Button What it does
Scanner (Capture Image From Scanner) Scans the card from the desk scanner
Database (Import id card image from file) Loads a JPG file instead
Magnifier (Zoom IN), or click the picture Shows the picture full size
Delete (Remove id image) Removes the picture

To scan: place the card on the scanner and click the scanner button. Mp10 crops the picture to the card by itself. The picture is stored when you save the patient, so press Save afterwards.

A patient has one ID picture. Scanning again replaces it.

For anything else — the back of the ID, a referral, a signed consent form — use Attachments in the patients grid’s side panel. There you can scan, import JPG or PDF files, scan several pages into one PDF (Scan to PDFs), and give each document a type and a date.

If the scanner does not respond, or the wrong scanner is used, ask your administrator: the scanner each workstation uses is a setting.

The HIPAA signature

The patient signs the practice’s HIPAA notice once, on the signature pad, and the signature is kept on their record. The HIPAA Signature box on the Demographics tab shows it, with the date and time it was taken underneath.

Taking it

  1. Save the patient first. The pen button stays disabled on a new, unsaved patient.
  2. Click the pen, Pickup Patient Signature using signing pad.
  3. The pad shows the practice’s notice, a page at a time. The patient taps the arrow on the pad to move to the next page. While they read, your screen shows Wait for user input with a Cancel button.
  4. The patient signs and taps OK on the pad (or CLEAR to start again).
  5. Your screen shows the signature in the Signature Dialog. Click Accept to keep it, or Cancel to throw it away.

Mp10 stores the signature, the time, who took it, and a fingerprint of the exact text the patient was shown. If the notice is reworded later, you can still tell which wording each patient signed.

Taking a signature again replaces the old one, without asking. Only press the pen when a new signature is wanted.

The printer button beside it, Print HIPAA documents with patient signature printed, prints the practice’s HIPAA document with the patient’s signature on it.

The text on the pad

What the pad shows is set once by the administrator, in the system registry under TOPAZ / Patient-Record-DisplayMessage. It can be plain text, or a more complete setting that also chooses the font, the pad model and a list of options for the patient to tap. The format is described in the Mp10 Web — Signature Pads guide, chapter Using it, and in the Mp10 — Signature Helper guide. Those guides describe the setting for the encounter signature; the HIPAA setting takes exactly the same format.

The dashboard’s Patient records card counts the patients seen in the last 90 days who have no HIPAA signature, so the ones missed are easy to find.

If the pad does not respond

Topaz signature device could not be activated or TOPAZ signature pad is not found or missing required components means this workstation’s pad is not connected or its software is not installed. Check the cable first; the installation is covered in the Mp10 Web — Signature Pads guide, chapter Installing it — IT task.

Insurance cards

The Insurance tab of the patient window lists every insurance card the patient has ever presented, newest and oldest together. Each row is one card. Below the list are the card’s details, and on the right, the picture of the card.

Why the card history matters

Patients change insurance. When they do, do not overwrite the old card with the new one. Add the new card as a new row, make it primary, and leave the old one on file. The old card is the record of who was billed for the earlier visits: when a payer asks about a claim from two years ago, or a payment arrives late for an old plan, the card that was in force then is still there, with its picture.

The fields

Field What to enter
Member Number The member or subscriber ID, exactly as on the card
Group The group number. Fill it in whenever the card shows one — see below
Insurance Co. The payer. Use the search button to pick from the payers your practice has set up
Effective Date, Exp Date When the coverage starts and ends
Insured First, Last Names The subscriber — the person who holds the policy
Relationship The patient’s relationship to the subscriber: Self, Spouse, Child, Other… Choosing Self fills in the subscriber’s name from the patient
DOB The subscriber’s date of birth, as the payer has it
Name as on card, Last as on card The name printed on the card, if it differs
Coverage Codes, Ded Amt Deductible information
Primary, Secondary Which payer is billed first and second

For Medicare, Mp10 checks that the member number looks like a Medicare Beneficiary Identifier and asks you to confirm if it does not.

The Group matters because when Mp10 writes the electronic claim it chooses, among the patient’s cards for that payer, the one that has a group number. A new card left without a group can mean the claim goes out with the old card’s details.

Adding a new card

  1. Save the patient if it is new — the Insurance tab works only on a saved patient.
  2. Click New.
  3. Fill in the fields.
  4. Scan the card (next section).
  5. Tick Primary if this is now the payer to bill first, or Secondary, or neither.
  6. Click Save.

To change a card, highlight it, click Modify, make the change and click Save. Cancel throws your changes away. Double-clicking a row is the same as Modify.

Making the newest card primary, and keeping the old one

When you save a card marked Primary, Mp10 takes the Primary mark off whichever card had it before. That card stays on file, unmarked, as history, with its picture. The same happens with Secondary. There is always at most one primary and one secondary card.

So, when a patient brings a new card:

  1. Click New and enter the new card.
  2. Tick Primary.
  3. Save.

The old card is now history and the new one is billed. If you know the old coverage ended, open the old card with Modify and enter its Exp Date. Mp10 does not set it for you.

What uses which card:

  • A new encounter takes the primary card’s payer.
  • A new claim takes the primary card’s payer and member number, and the secondary card’s payer as its complementary insurance.
  • An eligibility check asks the primary payer.
  • Existing encounters and claims keep the payer they were created with. Changing the primary card later does not change them.

Only Supervisors can delete a card. That is deliberate: a card that is no longer valid should be left as history, not deleted.

The picture of the card

Each card row keeps one picture of the card. While you are adding or modifying a card:

  • Scan captures it from the desk scanner. Mp10 crops to the card.
  • Import card image from file loads a JPG or PDF file.
  • Delete removes the picture.

The picture is stored when you save the card. To see it later, highlight the card in the list: its picture shows on the right. Preview, or a click on the picture, shows it full size.

A card row holds one picture, normally the front. Keep the back of the card, when you need it, in the patient’s Attachments.

The OCR button does not read the card for you; enter the details by hand.

Checking eligibility

Before the visit, ask the payer whether the patient is covered. In the patients grid, highlight the patient and click Eligibility in the side panel, or click the 271 icon in the Insurance column.

The Insurance Eligibility window lists the answers already received. Click Fetch New Eligibility record. to ask the primary payer now. Mp10 connects to the clearinghouse (Connecting with clearing house. Stand by) and adds the answer to the list.

If the payer’s answer differs from what you have on file — a different spelling, a different date of birth — Fix patient record with data from Eligibility. shows the patient record as the payer has it, with the differences highlighted. Accept saves those changes; Cancel leaves the record alone.

Your administrator can also have Mp10 ask automatically each time the primary member number changes.

Encounters

An encounter is one visit: one patient, one date, one payer. Everything that happens during the visit hangs from it — what was ordered, the patient’s signature, the questionnaire, and in the end, the claim. Mp10 numbers encounters EN followed by the year and a sequence, such as EN26-00012345.

Creating an encounter

  1. Open the patient (and save it, if it is new).
  2. Go to the Encounters tab.
  3. Click New (Insert New Case), or press Insert.

The encounter window opens over the patient. Its tabs are Encounter Data, Remarks, Orders and Questionnaires.

On Encounter Data:

Field What to enter
Encounter Number Filled in by Mp10 when you first save
Encounter type (the list at the top) The kind of visit, from your practice’s list
From The date of the visit. Tick the box and pick the date — a new encounter starts with no date
Thru The last day, for a visit that spans more than one
Referring Phys The referring doctor. Use the search button; only active doctors are offered
Insurance The payer for this visit. It starts as the patient’s primary card
Inactive encounter Tick it for a no-show (below)
Accident Is Auto Accident, Work related Accident or Is Other Accident, and the accident date, when the visit is the result of one

The referring doctor here becomes the referring doctor on the claim, and the accident boxes are sent on the electronic claim, so fill them in carefully.

Click Save. Mp10:

  • refuses if there is no date (Encounter date cannot be blank);
  • warns if the patient already has an encounter on the same date, and asks whether to continue — answer No if this is a duplicate;
  • gives the encounter its number.

The window stays open after Save, so you can go on to the orders. Exit closes it.

If Save says This encounter is open somewhere else, another user has the same encounter open. Ask them to close it, then press Save again; nothing was lost.

From the patient’s Encounters tab you can also print the encounter form (Print Case sheet) and a label (Print Case Label).

Institutional: in Patients10 the encounter window has the tabs Encounter, Other Information, DRG/APC and Remarks. The encounter type is required, and you can enter the pre-authorization, admitting diagnosis, department, source, weight and height. The encounter window prints its own form and label. Orders are entered from the Encounters grid, not from a tab (next chapter).

Encounters made from the calendar

In the appointment calendar (MpXtrmCal10), an appointment for someone who is not yet a patient has a Create New Patient Record button. It opens a new patient window filled in from the questionnaire the patient answered on-line, and when you save the patient, the appointment is linked to the new record.

The calendar does not create encounters. When the patient arrives, open them (the … button beside Medical Record on the appointment) and create the encounter on their Encounters tab. Once the encounter exists, the calendar shows the appointment as arrived.

Encounters dated in the future

You can create an encounter ahead of time, for a scheduled visit. The Billing dashboard shows these as Upcoming encounters pre-created and does not count them as unbilled.

No-shows: inactivating an encounter

When the patient does not come, do not leave the encounter as it is: it would sit on the unbilled list for ever. Open it, tick Inactive encounter and save. Inactive encounters are shown in grey and left out of the unbilled lists and the dashboard counts.

The Questionnaires tab

On-line questionnaires the patient filled in before the visit are linked to the encounter when you save it, by the patient’s record number or cell phone and the appointment date. The tab lists them; Open shows one. You can also send the patient a text message asking them to fill one in.

Orders: what the patient came for

An order records what the patient is in for — the study, test or procedure — picked from your practice’s catalogue. The Mp10 Orders guide explains orders in full: setting up the catalogue, labels, sending orders by HL7, results coming back. This chapter covers entering them on the encounter and what they do for billing.

Why the front desk should enter them

Orders are how the front desk tells the biller what happened. When the biller creates the claim for an encounter that has orders, the claim’s procedure lines start from them: one line per procedure code, dated on the day of the visit. The biller confirms them instead of reconstructing the visit from paperwork.

This applies to every kind of visit, not only to studies. If your catalogue has an item for a follow-up visit, an injection or a consultation, ordering it puts its code on the claim. Ask your administrator to add an item to the catalogue for every service the practice bills.

Orders also fill the Ordered column of the unbilled encounters list, so the billing backlog reads as work — “MRI KNEE RT, FOLLOW-UP” — not as a list of names.

Entering orders on the encounter

  1. Save the encounter once — the Orders tab works only on a saved encounter.
  2. Go to the Orders tab.
  3. Click New Order (or press Insert).
  4. In the Item cell, type the item code, or click the cell’s button to pick from the catalogue (the Pick list shows each item’s description and body part).
  5. Add as many orders as the visit needs.
  6. Click Save.

Delete Order removes the highlighted order when you next save. Print Label prints a label for a saved order.

When you save, Mp10 warns if the encounter now has the same study ordered twice, or if the payer has already refused this item for this patient. The orders are saved either way; the warning is for you to act on. The Mp10 Orders guide explains both warnings.

The patient’s Encounters tab, and the Encounters and Unbilled grids, also have an Orders link that opens a separate Orders for window over the same orders. It appears only when your practice has orders switched on.

Institutional: in Patients10, open the Encounters Table, highlight the encounter and click Orders in the side panel. On institutional claims the procedure lines come from the charges posted to the encounter, not from the orders.

The patient’s signature on the encounter

Besides the HIPAA signature, which is taken once, many practices have the patient sign at every visit: a consent, a financial agreement, an acknowledgement. That signature belongs to the encounter.

Taking it

  1. Open the patient and go to the Encounters tab.
  2. Highlight the encounter.
  3. Click the pen, Pickup Patient Signature using signing pad.
  4. The patient reads the text on the pad, page by page, taps an option if one is offered, signs and taps OK.
  5. Click Accept on your screen.

The signature shows in the Signature column of the encounter list. As with the HIPAA signature, Mp10 keeps who took it, when, the option the patient picked, and the exact text shown. Signing again replaces the earlier signature.

If the patient signed the on-line questionnaire before the visit, that signature is copied onto the encounter when you save it, unless the encounter already has one.

Showing your own text on the pad

What the patient reads before signing is the practice’s own text, set once by the administrator in the system registry under TOPAZ / Encounter-Signature-DisplayMessage. It can be:

  • plain text — the pad shows it as it is, wrapped and paged;
  • the path of a text file — the file is read each time, so the text can be changed without touching the registry;
  • a JSON setting — which also sets the pad model, the font and size, a list of options for the patient to choose from (for example I agree / I do not agree), and whether the options and the signature start on a page of their own.

The text can include the patient’s e-mail address with ^email, so the patient can confirm it while signing.

The Mp10 Web — Signature Pads guide, chapter Using it, gives the format with examples, and the Mp10 — Signature Helper guide covers every option. The same setting is used by the Windows programs and by Mp10 Web.

Finding encounters with no signature

The dashboard’s Encounters missing something card counts recent encounters with no patient signature. The Billing dashboard’s Needs attention card shows unsigned encounters from the last seven days.

Payments at the front desk

Copays and deposits taken at the desk are posted with Pat Payment in the patients grid’s side panel (Post patient payment). The Patient Payment Dialog opens on the New Payment tab.

  1. Choose the encounter in Enc. Num., or the claim in ClaimKey if one already exists. A patient with no encounter cannot be given a payment: create the encounter first.
  2. If the patient pays by check, type the check number in Chk Num.
  3. On each line, choose what the payment is for in Concept, type the amount — in Deductible if it counts toward what the patient owes on the claim, in NonDeductible if it is for something else, such as materials — and the Method (Cash, Check, ATH, Visa, M/C, Amex).
  4. Click Save, then Print for the receipt.

All the lines saved together share one receipt number. The Previous tab lists the patient’s earlier payments and reprints any receipt.

A payment taken before the claim exists is held on the encounter, and the claim shows it as Recvd @ Pay Wndw. Until the claim takes it over, the claim carries the tag Unattached Patient Payments.

Encounters that have not been billed

An encounter with no claim is a visit nobody has been paid for. The longer it waits, the closer it gets to the payer’s filing deadline.

In PrBill10, the Unbilled encounters button on the toolbar (or Tables → Unbilled Encounters) lists them. The dashboard’s Billing card shows how many there are in the last 90 days and the date of the oldest; click that line to open the list for those 90 days.

The list shows active encounters with no claim. Its columns include the patient, the date, the doctor, the payer, and Ordered: what the patient came for, from the orders.

  • The Filter panel narrows it by encounter type, payer and dates. Changes apply when you click Refresh.
  • Show Inactive Encounters includes no-shows; Show Only Unbilled, unticked, shows billed encounters too.
  • New Claim in the side panel creates the claim for the highlighted encounter (Part II). After you save the claim, click Refresh and the encounter leaves the list.

The Mp10 Dashboard guide, chapter Unbilled encounters, explains how to work through the list and when to inactivate rather than bill.

For a printed list, use Reports → Unbilled Report.

Institutional: Patients10 has no unbilled list of its own; use Reports → Unbilled there, or the Unbilled Encounters list in MpBill10.

Part II — Claims, coding and collections

Turning an encounter into a claim, coding it, the error tags, sending it, and following it with the status flags until it is paid.

Creating a claim

A claim is the bill sent to the payer for one encounter. Mp10 numbers claims CL followed by the year and a sequence, such as CL26-00012345.

Where to start

Claims are created from the encounter they bill:

  • From the unbilled list (the usual way): highlight the encounter and click New Claim.
  • From the Encounters grid (Tables → Encounters): highlight the encounter, click New Claim.
  • From the patient: on the patient’s Claims tab, click Create New Claim (or press Insert), then choose the encounter in the claim’s Encounter list.

The Claims grid itself has no New Claim button: a claim always starts from its encounter. Only BillingUsers and Administrators can create claims.

What is filled in for you

A new claim starts from what is already known:

  • Insurance and Card Number — the patient’s primary card.
  • Comp Ins — the patient’s secondary card’s payer, if there is one.
  • Serv/Date — the encounter’s date.
  • Referring Phys — the encounter’s referring doctor.
  • Facility — your practice’s facility.
  • Procedure lines — one line for each procedure code ordered on the encounter, dated on the visit date, with one unit, pointing at the first diagnosis.

A code the schedule does not carry for that payer stays at 0.00 — look at the Fee total before saving, and price any such line by picking the code in its BillCode cell.

Its tabs, in PrBill10, are Summary, Procs (procedures), ICD9, ICD10, Rmrks (remarks), Pymnts (payments), EncReps and PaprWrk (paperwork). The procedure lines appear on both Summary and Procs; they are the same lines.

Primary and secondary claims

The claim you create is always the primary claim. You do not create the secondary claim yourself. When the primary payer’s payment is posted and part of the balance is assigned to the complementary payer, Mp10 creates the secondary claim automatically, billing exactly what was assigned. See Complementary insurance claims in Part III.

A secondary claim opens with the bar Complementary Insurance Claim and does not let you change its lines.

Coding the diagnoses (ICD-10)

The ICD10 tab

The ICD10 tab (Alt+C) lists the claim’s diagnoses in order. The first diagnosis is the principal one — the reason for the visit.

  • Type a code straight into the IcdCode cell, with its dot (M17.11). If the code is valid, its description fills in. If it is not an exact code, the row turns red and the description of its category is shown instead.
  • New adds a row. Delete removes the highlighted one.
  • Move up and Move Down change the order.
  • Run Analizer checks every code on the claim (next section).

The diagnoses belong to the encounter, not to one claim: if an encounter has more than one claim, they share the list. Up to twelve are sent on the electronic professional claim; any after the twelfth are not.

Looking codes up

Click the small button inside the IcdCode cell to open the Diagnosis Table, the ICD-10 encoder.

  • Search by words: type them in Filter Keys on the left and click Run Filter. Tick Left, Right or Bilateral to narrow to one side.
  • Search by code: on the ICD10 Diagnosis tab, sort by the Icd10 column and type, or press F8.
  • Browse: open the chapters and sections in the Icd10 Chapters + Sections tab. Opening a subsection lists its codes.
  • Pick: double-click a code. It is added to the Coding box on the left. Pick as many as you need, then Close Encoder.

When the encoder closes, Mp10 checks the codes you picked and adds them to the claim. Along the way it may ask you:

  • A code is not billable — ICD10 Code seems invalid, with the reason: not an ICD-10-CM code, a header code that has more specific codes under it, retired, or not yet valid. Choose the more specific code.
  • An additional code may apply — ICD-10-CM says “use additional code”; tick the ones that apply.
  • A seventh character is needed — pick it from the list.
  • Excludes1 — two codes ICD-10-CM says are never coded together. Keep both only if the two conditions are unrelated.
  • Diagnosis sequence — a “code first” instruction asks for the codes the other way round. Move them.

Pointing lines at diagnoses

Each procedure line has a Diag column: which diagnoses justify it, by their position in the ICD10 list. 1 is the first diagnosis, 1,2 the first and second. Separate numbers with commas.

Two things to watch:

  • Picking a code on a line resets its Diag to 1. Set the pointers after the codes.
  • Reordering the diagnoses changes what the numbers mean. After moving a code up or down, check every line’s pointers.

Codes, prices and the fee schedule

Modifiers

Type modifiers after the code, separated by spaces: 76881 TC RT. Up to four are sent. If your fee schedule prices the code with its modifiers as a separate entry, that price is used; otherwise the base code’s price is. After the price fills in, check the modifiers are still on the line.

Checking a batch of claims before sending

In the Claims grid, select claims and click Analyze 837 in the side panel. Mp10 reviews them all and prints a list of warnings: missing or invalid codes, duplicate diagnoses, codes that need a seventh character or a side, missing place of service, the tags each claim carries. Nothing is changed or sent.

Saving the claim, and error tags

When you save

Click Save. Mp10:

  • refuses a new claim with no lines (Nothing to save);
  • warns if the same codes, dates and payer are already billed for this patient (Billing Codes, dates, and insurance seems to be duplicated) — answer No if it is a duplicate;
  • refuses a payer that is not set up in your insurance list;
  • gives a new claim its number;
  • runs the claim checks and tags the claim with what they find.

If anything fails, nothing is saved: The claim was NOT saved. Everything from this save has been rolled back, followed by the reason.

Error tags

A tag is a coloured label at the bottom of the claim window naming a problem a check found: Wrong Side ICD10, ICD10 Relation, NCCI Pair Needs Modifier, Denied Before, and others. Tags never stop you from saving or sending a claim. They tell you what a payer is likely to reject, before it does.

  • Hover a tag to see what it found.
  • Click it for the full explanation: which lines, which codes, and why.
  • To clear it, fix what it names and save. Each check runs again on every save and removes its own tag when the problem is gone.

Three checks offer to fix the problem for you as you save, and change nothing unless you answer Yes:

  • Insurance DOB Mismatch — the payer’s last eligibility answer gives a different date of birth. Yes writes the payer’s date on the patient’s insurance card, not on the patient.
  • Insurance Sex Mismatch — Yes updates the patient’s record.
  • Payer Not On Patient — the claim bills a payer the patient has no card for. Yes adds it to the patient’s insurances; check the member number and dates afterwards. Answer No if the claim is the one that is wrong.

Two tags learn from the remittances you post (Part III): Denied Before, when this payer has refused these codes often and recently, and Refused For This Patient, when the payer refused these very codes for this patient and has paid nothing for them since.

You can also add your own tag with the + button beside the tags, to mark a claim for yourself or a colleague.

The full list of checks, what each looks for and how to switch one off is in the Mp10 Dashboard guide, chapters Claims with error tags and The claim checks. The dashboard’s Needs attention card lists every claim tagged in the last seven days.

In the Claims grid, a claim with tags shows a tag icon in SrvDate and its row is coloured orange.

Sending claims

Electronically (837)

  1. In the Claims grid, select the claims to send. They must all be for the same payer; filter by Ins first.
  2. Optionally, run Analyze 837 and correct what it finds.
  3. Click Outbound 837. Mp10 writes the claims file and shows a report of every claim in it, with the file name and any warnings at the end.
  4. Answer Yes to Send claims file to clearinghouse.

Each claim records, in its notes, the name of the file it went out in. That file name is your batch number.

Outbound 837 also collects whatever the clearinghouse has waiting — acknowledgements, claim statuses and remittances — and processes it, so the flags in the grid are up to date afterwards.

Supervisors and Administrators also have Actions → Send Outbound 837 Claims, which lists the claims files waiting in the outbound folder and sends them, and Actions → Fetch X12 Inbound Responses, which collects and processes the clearinghouse’s files on demand. Normally AutoTasks does both on a schedule; see the Mp10 AutoTasks guide.

On paper

Print Claim in the Claims grid prints the claim form — CMS-1500 or UB-04 — for every selected claim; Preview Claim shows it first. Actions → Print Claims prints all the claims of a date range for the payers you tick. Which form a payer gets is set on the payer’s record (Form #). A printer whose name contains FORM is used without asking.

Several things about a claim are decided not on the claim but on the payer’s record — the insurance company, under Tables → Insurance Companies (or in the administration program). Status flags: the rebilling and collections system

Sending a claim is the start, not the end. Payers acknowledge it, reject it, pay part of it or all of it, sometimes months apart. Mp10 keeps every one of those answers on the claim as its status history, and shows the latest as a coloured flag. The flags turn the Claims grid into a work queue: filter by flag and you have the list of claims that need you.

The flags

Flag Name Means Set by
Green Fully Paid Nothing left for the insurance to pay Posting a payment or remittance
Yellow Partially Paid (also Pending) Something paid, something still owed Posting; a payer status saying it is pending
Red Rejected Refused, by the clearinghouse or the payer, or paid nothing on the remittance Claim status responses; posting a remittance that pays nothing
Grey Received by Ins Accepted by the clearinghouse or payer, not paid yet Claim status responses and acknowledgements
Black OverPaid More was paid than billed Posting; also a payer status saying the claim was not found
Blue, checkered, pink, purple User Def 1–4 Your practice’s own meanings You
None No status Nothing heard yet —

The four user flags can be renamed for your practice — Appealed, Called payer, Sent to collections — by your administrator, in the system registry under BILLING / UserDefined-1-ClaimStatus to -4-.

Where flags come from

  • Claim status responses (277) and the clearinghouse’s acceptance reports arrive automatically and add a row to the history, with the payer’s words. They move the flag only on a claim that has no status, is rejected or is received. They never move a claim that is paid, partly paid, overpaid or carries one of your own flags.
  • Posting a remittance or a payment works out the flag from the balance: green when nothing is left, yellow when something is, red when nothing was paid, black when overpaid. A payment that settles the claim always turns it green.
  • You, through the status history or Change Flags (below).

The status history

Press F9 on a claim in the Claims grid (or right-click its ClaimKey, or use Claim Status). The Claim status history window lists every status the claim has had, oldest first: the flag, the date it was received, what was said, who recorded it and when.

The Data column holds the payer’s or clearinghouse’s own words for status responses, Remittance Posting. for remittances, Payment Posted by user. for payments keyed by hand. The button in the cell shows it in full, and its Print prints a status response as a readable form.

To record a follow-up — a call to the payer, an appeal sent, a promise to reprocess:

  1. Click Add New most recent status to list.
  2. Choose the flag.
  3. Set the date.
  4. Type a note in Data — what was done, who you spoke to, what was promised. A row saved without one reads Changed by user manually, which tells the next person nothing; write the note.
  5. Click Save.

The new row becomes the claim’s flag. Earlier rows cannot be changed, so the history is a true record of what happened when.

To change the flag of many claims at once, select them in the grid and click Claim Status. The Change Claim Status window lists the flags; tick one and click Change Flags. Each claim gets the new flag and a history row Changed by user manually. This is also the only way to clear a flag back to no status.

Mp10 has no follow-up date. The date on the history row, and the Days column of the Claims Status report (below), show how long a claim has been waiting since its last status.

Working the queue

A routine that keeps money from slipping past filing deadlines:

1. Rejections, every day. The dashboard’s Billing card shows the number of rejected claims in the last 90 days. Click it for the Rejected claims list: each claim with the date it was rejected, where the rejection came from — 277/CH for the clearinghouse or payer, 835 for a remittance — and the reason, in the payer’s words or as the remittance’s reason codes. Double-click a claim to open it, correct it, and resubmit (next chapter). Click Refresh and corrected claims leave the list.

2. Claims with no answer. A claim that has heard nothing at all after a couple of weeks may never have reached the payer. Filter the Claims grid by Missing Flag and a range of bill dates, and check those claims. The dashboard shows the count of primary claims with no status and the date of the oldest.

3. Claims stuck in received or partly paid. Reports → Claims Status lists claims with an insurance balance by flag and payer, with Days — how long each has sat on its current flag. Tick Received and Pending, read the oldest first, and call or appeal. Record each call in the status history so the next person knows. The report lists only claims that have a flag; use the grid’s Missing Flag filter for the ones with none.

4. Dollars by age. Reports → Aeging of Accounts totals what is owed by age — 30, 60, 90 days and beyond — for insurance and for patients, by payer or by financial class. Use it to see where the money is waiting, and the Claims Status report to work it.

5. What the patient owes. When the insurance has paid its part, the rest is the patient’s: see Collecting from the patient.

Correcting and resubmitting a claim

When a payer rejects or underpays a claim, correct the claim and send it again, telling the payer which claim it replaces.

  1. Correct the claim. Open it, fix what the rejection names, save. Check the tags.
  2. Set the submission code. In the Claims grid, highlight the claim and press F4. In Claim Submission Code, choose:
    • Corrected or Replacement — this claim replaces the one the payer has (frequency code 6 or 7);
    • Void — cancel the claim the payer has (frequency code 8);
    • Original Claim — a first submission (the default).
    Click Save. The claim’s row turns cyan, and the claim window’s bar starts with Corrected:, Replacement: or Void:.
  3. Check the ICN. A corrected, replacement or void claim must carry the payer’s number for the original claim, its ICN (on the Summary tab). When the original was paid or denied on an electronic remittance you posted, Mp10 has already filled it in. When you type it yourself from a paper EOB, type F8 before the number — F8 followed by the ICN, with no space — or it is not sent as the original claim’s number. Mp10 does not warn you if the ICN is missing; the payer will reject the claim.
  4. Send it with Outbound 837, or print it.

This is the first reason to post remittances whole: they bring the ICN onto the claim for you. Part III explains the others.

Collecting from the patient

Once the insurance has paid, what remains on the claim is the patient’s balance, shown in the claim’s Pat Balance and in the Claims grid’s PatBal.

Collection letters. Actions → Pat Collection Letters lists the claims with a patient balance for a range of dates — service dates or billing dates, whichever box you tick — at or above the Minimum Debt to print collection letter you enter. For each claim it shows how many days it has been since the last letter, and which letter that was. Tick Also Print Next Corresponding Letter to print each patient their next letter: the first letter for a patient who has had none, and the next one in the series once 30 days have passed since the last. Your practice can have several letters, each firmer than the last; how many is set by BILLING / MAXIMUM_PATIENT_COLLECTION_LETTERS (three by default), and a patient who has had the last one is not printed again. Every letter printed is recorded on the patient’s Letters tab.

One patient at a time. On the patient’s Claims tab, Current Patient Bal shows what they owe in total, and Print Patient Statement prints a collection letter for them. The patients grid’s Prnt Statement prints a full statement of every service.

Taking the payment is described in Payments at the front desk (Part I), or with Pat Payment in the Claims grid, which prints a receipt.

Part III — Remittances and payments

Receiving, reading and posting the payer’s remittances, the other ways to enter a payment, and why the remittance is the one to use.

What a remittance is

A remittance is the payer’s explanation of one payment: one check or electronic transfer, and every claim it pays, denies or adjusts. For each claim it says what was billed, what was allowed, what was paid, what the patient owes, and why anything was not paid, in standard reason codes. It also carries the payer’s own number for each claim, the ICN.

Mp10 receives remittances in two forms:

  • Electronic remittances (X12 835) come from the clearinghouse. AutoTasks collects and imports them on a schedule.
  • PDF remittances (EOB or ERA PDFs downloaded from a payer’s website) are saved into a folder that AutoTasks watches. It reads them and imports them.

Both end up in the same place, the Remittances window, as remittances waiting to be posted. Importing posts nothing: no claim balance changes until someone posts the remittance. Setting up both is described in the Mp10 AutoTasks guide, chapters Electronic remittances: the two kinds, X12 835 electronic remittances and Electronic remittances (EOB / ERA PDFs).

A paper EOB that arrives by mail can be entered by hand in the same window: see Entering a remittance by hand.

Finding a remittance

Click Remittances Data Entry Dialog on the toolbar (the check), or Tables → Remittances. The window has two tabs:

  • Remittances — the list of remittances.
  • Detailed Check Tree View — one remittance, open for posting.

The list

The Remittances tab lists one row per check, with its Check number, Insurance, ChkDate (check date), RcvDate, Deposit date, ChkTotal, Amount, Retained, whether it is posted, the file it came from and the number of lines. A green dot beside the check number means posted; a red dot means waiting to be posted.

In the Remittances Period panel on the left, narrow the list by check dates (From, Thru), by payer (Insurance, any part of the name), or tick Show Only Posting Pending for the ones still to post. Then click Reload Remits in the Actions panel.

To find a check number or an amount, sort by the Check or ChkTotal column and look down the list.

Posting a remittance

Before you post

The import fills in what the payer paid, the deductible and coinsurance, the reason codes and the ICN. Most write-offs it leaves to you:

Contractual adjustments. The difference between your charge and what the payer allows (reason code CO-45, and others like it) has to be written off before the claim balances. To write off what is left on a line, right-click its Adj cell. On a claim row, the same writes off every line. To do the whole check at once, click the Adj column header: Maximum Amount To Adjust writes off every paid claim whose remaining balance is no more than the amount you give. Read the reason codes first — not everything unpaid should be written off.

Deductibles. Click the Deduct column header to choose what posting does with deductibles: Add to patient responsiblity moves them to the patient’s balance (the default), Ignore Deductibles leaves them out.

Coinsurance for the complementary payer. When the claim has a complementary payer, what is in Deduct and CoIns is billed to it (see below).

The ICN. Check it is there; you can type it if it is missing.

Then watch Diff.: when it is zero, every cent of the check is accounted for.

Posting

  1. Click Save Remit (Ctrl+S).
  2. Click Post Remit in the Actions panel (or right-click → Post Remittance).

A progress bar runs, and Mp10 reports how many entries it posted: Remittance has been posted to payments table.

Posting a check, in one step that either completes or changes nothing:

  • records a payment, an adjustment and the reason codes on every claim line;
  • recalculates each claim’s balances;
  • sets each claim’s flag — green, yellow, red or black — and adds a Remittance Posting. row to its status history;
  • moves deductibles to the patient’s balance, as you chose;
  • creates or updates the complementary claims (below);
  • records the payer’s refusals in the denial history that the claim checks learn from.

Only lines that are not yet posted are posted. Posting the same check again posts nothing: Already posted. If Mp10 answers Nothing posted, the check number, payer or date on the left do not match the ones the remittance was saved with — correct them and post again.

There is no undo

A posted remittance cannot be unposted. Check Diff. and the dots before you post. A mistake found afterwards is corrected on the claim, with the payment window (see Other ways to enter a payment).

An Administrator can delete a remittance that has not been posted, from the right-click menu of the Remittances list (Delete Remittance): for instance, a check entered twice by hand.

Complementary insurance claims

When the patient has a second insurance that pays after the first (the claim’s Comp Ins), posting the primary payer’s remittance hands the rest over:

  • The amounts in Deduct and CoIns are assigned to the complementary payer instead of the patient.
  • If there is no secondary claim yet, Mp10 creates it: a claim for the complementary payer, billing exactly what was assigned, with the patient’s secondary card. Send it like any other claim.
  • If the secondary claim already exists, it is adjusted to the new amount.

Posting a check twice never creates a second secondary claim.

When a claim is not found

A grey dot means Mp10 could not tell which claim the payer was paying — the payer quoted a number Mp10 does not know. Those lines do not post; the rest of the check does, and the check stays in the list as partly pending.

To fix one:

  • Type the right claim number (or the encounter number) in its ClaimKey / Bill-Code cell; the claim’s lines load. Save and post again.
  • Re-Fetch Missing Claims (right-click in the Remittances list) tries the match again from the original file — useful after a patient or claim has been corrected.
  • Change Insurance Info moves the whole check to the right payer, when it was filed under the wrong one.
  • Re-Fetch From 835 throws away an unposted remittance and imports it again from its file.

Entering a remittance by hand

A paper EOB is entered in the same window, so that it is posted, reconciled and remembered exactly like an electronic one.

  1. Click Start New Remit.
  2. On the left, enter Chk Num, the payer (Ins), Chk Date, and Claims Pd — the total the EOB says was paid on claims. Add PLB Adj if the payer withheld or added money outside the claims.
  3. Add the claims on the EOB:
    • press Insert (or right-click → Add New Line) and type the claim number in ClaimKey / Bill-Code: the claim’s lines load; or
    • click Fetch Claims Set to load many claims at once — all the unpaid claims of the ticked payers for a range of dates, or a list of claim numbers you type in.
  4. On every line, enter what the EOB says. Mp10 fills Paid with the expected amount to save typing, so correct each one that differs. Enter Adj, Deduct, CoIns, and the ICN from the EOB.
  5. Enter the reason codes in AdjCodes, one per line, as [45]- followed by the description. Codes entered this way count toward the denial history, like the ones that come in electronically.
  6. When Diff. is zero, Save Remit, then Post Remit.

Other ways to enter a payment

A payment on one claim

Post Payment in the Claims grid, or Add New Payment to Claim on the claim’s Pymnts tab, opens the Single Payment Dialog. The claim must be saved first.

On the New Payment tab, enter the Check #, Check Date and Deposit Date (they are remembered for the next claim), then, on each procedure line:

Column Enter
InsPaid What the insurance paid
Substract The insurance write-off for the line
PtePaid What the patient paid
PSubstct The patient write-off
CoIns What to bill to the complementary payer
AdjReason Why, from your list of adjustment reasons

The buttons above the grid fill columns for you: the deductible as paid, the expected as paid, the insurance balance as coinsurance. The cell buttons do the same line by line.

The Simple Adjust tab adds to or subtracts from the whole claim’s insurance or patient balance in one entry, with a reason and a note. Save simple adjustments separately: when any Simple Adjust amount is filled in, the line payments on the New Payment tab are not saved in the same Save.

The Previous tab lists everything already posted to the claim.

When you save, the claim’s balances and flag are recalculated, and a secondary claim is created if you assigned coinsurance and the claim has a complementary payer.

Patient payments

Payments from the patient are posted with Pat Payment — from the patients grid at the front desk (Part I), or from the Claims grid, which ties the payment to that claim and prints a receipt.

Adjusting balances in bulk

Actions → Adjust Accts (Supervisors and Administrators) writes off small remaining balances across many claims: by dates of service, a range of balances, the payers you tick, and a reason. It first prints the list of claims it would adjust, and asks you to confirm before it changes anything — Create report only is ticked to begin with, so a first run only shows you. Adjusted claims are marked paid.

Refunds

Mp10 has no refund function. An overpaid claim shows a black flag; ask your administrator how your practice records a refund.

Why post whole remittances

Typing a check’s payments claim by claim in the payment window balances the claims, but it leaves out everything else the remittance knows. Posting the remittance itself — electronic, PDF, or a paper EOB entered in the Remittances window — gives you four things the payment window cannot:

The ICN, for corrections. The remittance carries the payer’s number for every claim, and Mp10 puts it on the claim. A corrected or replacement claim must quote it, or the payer rejects it as a duplicate. Posted from a remittance, it is already there when you need it.

The denial history. Every refusal on a posted remittance is remembered — which payer, which codes, which reason — and the claim checks learn from it. The Denied Before tag warns the biller, before the claim goes out, that this payer often refuses these codes. Refused For This Patient warns, at the front desk when the service is ordered and again on the claim, that the payer already refused it for this patient. The dashboard’s rejected claims list explains each remittance denial with its reason codes. All of this reads the remittances only. A denial typed into the payment window is invisible to it, and the next claim goes out with the same mistake.

Reconciliation. The remittance must add up to the check: Diff. shows you, before you post, whether every cent is accounted for, and PLB Adj explains what the payer withheld. The payment window has no check total to compare against.

The flags and the secondary claims. Posting sets every claim’s flag and status history in one step, and creates each secondary claim with exactly the amounts the primary payer assigned.

So: post every remittance whole, and use the payment window for what is not on a remittance.
