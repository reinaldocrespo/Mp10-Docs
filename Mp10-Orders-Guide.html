About this guide

An order records what a patient is in for: the study, test or procedure, picked from your practice’s catalogue and attached to the patient’s encounter. An encounter can carry one order or several.

This guide is for everyone an order touches: the front desk or admissions staff who enter them, the billers who use them, and the administrator who sets up the catalogue and the interfaces. It explains why orders are worth entering, how to enter them, how they reach the biller, how they print as a label, how they can be sent by HL7 to other systems, and how results coming back are matched to them.

Some practices enter orders on every encounter because they must: their imaging or reporting systems learn about each study from Mp10’s orders. Others do not enter them at all. The next chapter makes the case that every practice should.

Why enter orders

Entering an order takes a few seconds at registration. It can feel like extra typing nobody asked for. It is not, because of what happens to it afterwards.

  • The claim starts itself. When a biller creates a claim for an encounter that has orders, in the professional billing module or in MpWeb, the claim’s procedure lines are already filled in from them: the procedure codes and the date of the visit. Without orders, every line is typed from scratch, from whatever paperwork reached the biller.
  • The biller knows what the patient came for. The order is the record of the visit’s purpose, available to whoever bills it, wherever they sit. A biller working remotely, in MpWeb or in the desktop modules, has the same information the front desk had, without a phone call or a scanned sheet.
  • Unbilled visits say what is waiting to be billed. The unbilled encounters list shows, beside each patient, what they came for, so the billing backlog reads as work, not as a list of names.
  • A label prints from it. Each order, or the encounter as a whole, prints a label naming the patient and what they are having done: for envelopes, specimen bags, film jackets or folders.
  • Mistakes are caught early. When an order is saved, Mp10 warns if the same study was ordered twice, or if the patient’s insurance has already refused that procedure for this patient. Both are far cheaper to fix at the front desk than as a rejected claim weeks later.
  • Other systems can be told. Orders can be sent by HL7 to an imaging or reporting system, and listed on Mp10’s own modality worklist so the scanners know who is next. Results sent back are filed against the order they answer.

None of this happens for an encounter without orders. Billing it still works exactly as before; it is simply slower, and blind to everything above.

Setting up the order catalogue

Orders are picked from a catalogue of order items, which the administrator maintains in Admin10 → Order Items. Each item has:

  • an item code — what the front desk types or picks. It can be the procedure code itself (72040) or a code of your own (IT18-00000136).
  • a description — what everyone sees, for example NECK SONOGRAM.
  • a CPT, with modifiers when the item is side-specific (73718 LT). This is what the claim is built from, so it must be right.
  • a body part, used to tell apart studies that share a CPT.
  • a revenue code, which also decides the modality on the worklist (US, CT, MR and so on).

Several items may share one CPT when they are different studies: a neck and a thyroid sonogram are both 76536, and a wrist, elbow and biceps ultrasound are all 76881 RT. Give each its own body part, and Mp10 will not mistake them for duplicates.

Creating orders

Orders can be entered in three places. All three write to the same place, so an order entered in one shows in the others.

On the encounter, as the patient is registered

This is where orders are normally entered. In a professional practice the encounter is opened from the patient’s Encounters tab, in PrBill10 or in the appointment calendar, and the encounter window has an Orders tab.

The institutional Patients module (Patients10) has no Orders tab on its encounter window. There, open the Encounters Table, highlight the encounter and choose Orders in the side panel; the window that opens is the one described under From the Billing module below.

  1. Open the patient’s encounter and save it if it is new. Orders can only be added to an encounter that has been saved.
  2. Choose the Orders tab.
  3. Choose New Order (or press Insert). A new row opens.
  4. In the Item cell, type the item code, or choose the button in the cell to pick from the catalogue. The pick list shows the item, its description, body part and modality; typing a code that is not in the catalogue opens the same list so you can choose.
  5. Repeat for each study the patient is having.
  6. Save the encounter. The orders are saved with it.

To take an order off, select it and choose Delete Order (or press Delete), then save the encounter.

When the encounter is saved, Mp10 may show one of two messages about the orders you just added. Neither stops the save; the orders are already written.

  • Duplicate Order — the same study is now ordered more than once on this encounter: the same item twice, or two items with the same procedure code on the same body part (for example LEFT HIP 2-3 VIEWS and LEFT HIP AP AND FROG). Right and left are different studies, and so are different body parts sharing a code, so those never warn. If one order is extra, delete it and save again.
  • Refused For This Patient — the patient’s insurance has already refused this procedure for this same patient, and has not paid it since. The message names the date and the payer’s own reason, such as a missing authorization or credentialing. Check what the reason names before the service is billed. The claim created later carries the same warning as a tag.

The same two messages appear wherever orders are entered: on this tab, in the Billing module’s orders window, and in MpWeb.

From the Billing module

A biller can add or correct orders without leaving billing. In the Unbilled Encounters grid, an encounter that has orders shows an orders icon beside its date; choose the icon, or Orders in the task panel, to open the encounter’s orders.

The window lists the catalogue on the left and the encounter’s orders on the right. Select an item and choose the right arrow to add it, or select an order and choose the left arrow to remove it, then save. The filter button narrows a long catalogue. Saving gives the same duplicate-order and refused-for-this-patient messages as the encounter’s Orders tab.

In MpWeb

Billers and staff working remotely can manage orders in the browser. Open the Orders screen from the patient’s Encounters tab, the Encounters list, or Manage orders on the encounter itself. The catalogue is on the left and the encounter’s orders on the right; the arrows move items across, and nothing is saved until you choose Save orders. If the save added a duplicate study, or a code the payer has refused for this patient, the dialog says so in an amber box above the lists; the orders are saved either way. The Mp10 Web — User Guide describes the screen in full, including why an order that already has a result cannot be removed.

How orders reach the biller

The claim is pre-filled

When a biller creates a new claim for an encounter in the professional billing module (PrBill10), Mp10 adds one procedure line for each code the encounter’s orders carry, with the visit date as the service dates and one unit, priced from the payer’s fee schedule as of that date. The biller completes the rest: diagnoses, pointers, modifiers the order did not carry, and the price of any code the schedule does not have for that payer. A code ordered twice still makes one line. The institutional billing module (MpBill10) does not pre-fill claim lines from orders; its billers still see the orders on the encounter.

MpWeb’s new-claim screen does the same, and also prices the lines from the payer’s contract. It leaves out any order already billed on another claim of the same encounter, so a second claim never repeats the first one’s lines.

Unbilled encounters show what was ordered

  • The Unbilled Encounters grid has an Ordered column, right after the patient’s name, listing what each patient came for. A study ordered twice shows as, for example, CERVICAL SPINE XR x2, so a duplicate is visible at a glance. The orders icon beside the date opens the encounter’s orders.
  • MpWeb’s Encounters list has the same Ordered column. If you had arranged that grid’s columns before, the new column may appear at the far right; drag it where you want it, or use Reset layout.
  • The Unbilled report in the Billing module’s reports lists, for each unbilled encounter, every item ordered with its procedure code and description, and the first lines of any transcription.
  • The dashboard’s Today card counts the orders entered on today’s encounters, so a day with visits and no orders stands out.

Printing labels

Two labels print from orders:

  • The order label. On the encounter’s Orders tab, select a saved order and choose Print Label. It prints the patient, the encounter, the doctor and that order, and lists every test ordered on the encounter. The Billing module’s orders window has the same button, Print Order Label. An order must be saved before its label can print.
  • The encounter label. On the encounter window, Print label with encounter prints the encounter label, which also lists the tests ordered.

Use them on envelopes, specimen bags, film jackets or folders: anywhere the patient’s name and what they are having done must travel together.

Each label opens as a preview; print it from there to the label printer. The layouts are report forms your reseller can adapt.

Sending orders out by HL7

Mp10 can tell another system — an imaging system, a reporting service, an interface engine — about each order as it is entered, using standard HL7 ORM (order) messages. When an order is deleted, a cancel message goes out instead.

How it works

  1. When an order is saved, a database trigger queues a message for it. When an order is deleted, the trigger queues a cancel, unless a result has already come back for that order; then the delete is refused, because the order is part of the clinical record.
  2. The HL7 client service picks up the queue, builds each message from the patient, the encounter, the doctor and the order item, and writes it as a file into a folder.
  3. The receiving system, or an interface engine in front of it, collects the files from that folder.

The message carries Mp10’s order number (for example OR26-00052073) in the ORC segment. That same number is how results coming back are matched to the order; see the next chapter.

Files, not a network connection. The service writes each message to a file in the folder named below. Sending straight to a network address is not active in the current build, so the receiving side must collect the files. Without a folder set, nothing is delivered.

Turning it on

All settings are in Admin10 → Global System Registry. Note that the two sections are spelled differently: one has a dash after HL7, the other does not. Copy them exactly.

Section Entry Value
HL7ORM-TRANSMIT ACTIVATE YES
HL7-ORM-TRANSMIT FILEPATH The folder the receiving system collects from, for example D:\HL7\Outbound\

Then:

  1. Create the folder and make sure the account the HL7 client service runs as can write to it, and the receiving system can read from it.
  2. Install the triggers. In Admin10, run Update Data Dictionary (in the Actions panel). Among its steps it installs the two triggers that queue a message whenever an order is added or deleted. Run it with the other modules closed. Orders entered before this step are not queued.
  3. Install and start the HL7 client service on the server — an IT task. From a command prompt in the Mp10 folder, run MpHl7.exe I once to install it, then start Structured Systems HL7 Client Service from Windows Services. MpHl7.exe U uninstalls it.
  4. Restart the service after changing any of the settings above; it reads them when it starts.

Checking it is working

  1. Add an order to a test encounter and save it.
  2. Within a couple of minutes a new message file appears in the folder.
  3. The service’s log, MpHl7.out beside MpHl7.exe, records each message written and the file it went to.

If no file appears, check in this order: the service is running; ACTIVATE is YES under HL7ORM-TRANSMIT; FILEPATH is set under HL7-ORM-TRANSMIT and the folder exists; Update Data Dictionary has been run since ACTIVATE was set.

Results coming back

When the receiving system finishes a study — the radiologist reads the images, the report is signed — it can send the result back to Mp10 as an HL7 ORU (result) message. Mp10’s HL7 server, the MpSocketServ service, receives it and files it against the order.

How a result finds its order

  1. The sending system connects to MpSocketServ on the address and port configured for it, and sends the result.
  2. MpSocketServ stores the message as it arrives, then acknowledges it, so nothing received is ever lost. A message sent twice with the same message id is acknowledged again but stored once.
  3. It reads the order number from the message’s ORC segment — the same Mp10 order number that went out in the order message — and matches it to the order.
  4. The result becomes a transcription on that order’s encounter, with the ordering and performing doctors, and the order is now part of the clinical record: it can no longer be removed from the encounter.

The order number is the only link. A result whose order number Mp10 does not recognise, or that carries none, is stored but not filed, and waits; after repeated attempts it is set aside as unprocessable, so it does not retry forever.

Where results appear

  • In MpWeb, select the encounter and choose Results to view or print it.
  • In the desktop modules, the transcription is on the encounter, and the Unbilled Encounters grid shows an icon beside the patient’s name on encounters that have one.

The settings

The HL7 server’s settings are in the HL7-SERVER section of the System Registry. Setting it up is an IT task, done once with whoever runs the sending system.

Entry Default Meaning
IP 0.0.0.0 The address MpSocketServ listens on; 0.0.0.0 means every address of the server
PORT 6261 The port the sending system connects to
ACK_EXPECTED YES Reply to each message with an HL7 acknowledgement
ACK_SENDING_APP MP10 The name Mp10 gives itself in its acknowledgements
ACK_SENDING_FACILITY empty The facility name in its acknowledgements
MAX_PROCESS_ATTEMPTS 5 Attempts to file a result before it is set aside
RAW_JOURNAL_DAYS 7 Days the raw incoming messages are kept. They contain patient data

The modality worklist

Imaging scanners can ask Mp10 who is next. AutoTasks turns the day’s imaging orders into a modality worklist that the scanners query, each seeing its own studies. The modality comes from the order item’s revenue code.

There is nothing extra to enter per order: an order on an open, active encounter within the worklist’s date window is on the list. Setting up the worklist server and the scanners is described in the Mp10 Modality Worklist guide, and its AutoTasks settings in the Mp10 AutoTasks guide.
